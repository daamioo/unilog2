    Working the UNILOG-2 bench

    UNILOG-2 simulator by daamioo

    The case works like the one in the lab. Modules sit in 12 slots. A module works only once it has power: cable its +5V (VCC) and 0V (GND) pins to the rail. Until then its outputs are dead. Wire power in the inspector does it for you, and the design builder cables power for every module it uses. Every chip pin has two header pins, and every header takes one dupont cable. Chip inputs that aren't connected float HIGH, the way TTL does. The display register LEDs stay dark until a signal drives them HIGH.

    Wiring

    • Click a pin, then click another pin to run a cable. Dragging from pin to pin also works.
    • Drag a cable end off its pin to move it. Release it on empty space to unplug it.
    • Click a cable to select it. Remove it with Delete, a double-click, or the button in the inspector.
    • Esc cancels a cable you're placing.

    Modules

    • Drag a board from the module rack (on the table left of the case) onto a slot, or click it to drop it in the first free slot. On an empty slot it goes in. On a module with no cables it replaces that module. A module with cables on it stays put: unplug them first.
    • Drag a module that's already in the case to move it. Dropping it on another module swaps the two, and the cables go with them. Drop it outside the case to put it back on the rack.
    • Click a module to see its pinout and whether it is powered. R turns it 180°.
    • The splitter board (at the end of the rack) has 14 rows of 4 pins, and every row is one point. Use it when a signal must reach more places than a pin's two headers allow, for example one gate output feeding four LEDs. The design builder uses it for that too.

    Panel

    • Rail between the rows: 0V, H and +5V, once per column. The UNILOG-2 manual calls the H pins "constant logic levels": they give a logic 1 for inputs that must stay HIGH. In the simulator the three H pins of a column are one point, so they also work as a junction: plug an output in, and the other two H pins carry that output (it overrides the logic 1). Wiring +5V straight to 0V trips the supply.
    • Switch register: 8 latching buttons, each with Q and Q̄ outputs. Keys 1–8 toggle them too.
    • Pulse: 3 momentary buttons with ⊓ (HIGH while held) and ⊔ (LOW while held) outputs.
    • Clock: pick 1 Hz, 1 kHz or 1 MHz. CAL trims the rate (drag it or use the scroll wheel). 1 MHz runs in slow motion so the simulation can keep up.
    • Display: segment inputs a–g and DP are active-high. If any of the 2⁰–2³ inputs is wired, the built-in decoder shows that hex digit.
    • Oscilloscope: wire a signal to the A or B pin next to the BNC sockets. They are cabled to the scope on the table behind this pamphlet. Press its red POWER button (or click a BNC socket on the case) to switch it on, and click its screen to look at it up close. The TIME / DIV knob sets the sweep: click for slower, Shift+click for faster, or scroll over it. TRIG switches between triggering on a rising edge of A and a free-running roll.

    The sheets in the lid

    • Two A4 sheets are tucked into the pocket inside the lid: the logic diagram on the left and the truth table on the right. They are the real tools, not printouts: everything on them can be clicked. Click the blank part of a sheet to read it up close, and click its heading (or press Esc or Bench) to step back. Logic view or L goes straight to the diagram.
    • The diagram sheet shows the designer while the case is empty (or while the bench is the one built from your design), and the wired circuit once you wire it by hand. Open the designer and Show the wired circuit switch between the two.
    • The wired circuit: sources sit on the left, gates in the middle in order of signal depth, and LEDs, the display and the scope on the right. Feedback paths, like the cross-coupling in a latch, loop underneath. Wires are red when HIGH, green when LOW, dashed when floating, and blinking amber when two outputs fight.
    • Click a switch, pulse or the clock on the diagram to operate it. Click a gate to select its module on the bench.
    • The designer: draw a circuit from ideal gates (AND, OR, NAND, NOR, XOR, XNOR, NOT with 2–8 inputs), switches, pulses, the clock, constants, LEDs and the hex display. Drag from an output dot to an input dot to wire them.
    • Build on bench runs the solver. It breaks the gates into real 74xx gates and rewrites them so they fit the chips already in the case (AND becomes NAND + NOT, OR goes through NANDs, and so on), cancels double inverters, and packs the result into as few modules as possible. Missing chips come from the rack. It then lays every dupont cable within the real limits (two headers per pin, one cable per header) and lists them so you can copy the wiring onto the real case. Undo on the top bar restores the bench as it was.

    Truth tables

    • The truth table sheet measures a hand-wired bench by itself whenever the wiring changes, except for circuits with memory, where stepping the switches would change what a latch or counter holds. Design and Bench fill it on demand: from your design (ideal gates), or by measuring the bench: it steps through every combination of the wired switches and pulses, reads the LEDs, then puts the switches back. Circuits with memory are evaluated top to bottom, so each row follows on from the one above.
    • Click an output cell to cycle it through 0, 1 and X (don't care). The minimised expression for each output updates as you edit (Quine–McCluskey).
    • Create design turns the table into gates and opens it in the designer. Tick and build it on the bench to go straight from a truth table to modules and cables.
    • The Smart style designs the way a person at the bench does. It spots XOR patterns (parity, Gray code) and chains them, reuses outputs and signals it has already built (one output can be a single gate fed by two earlier ones), and tries short tricks of up to three gates. Otherwise it falls back to minimised NAND–NAND or NOR–NOR. It designs for the chips in your case: without a 7408 or 7432, an AND or OR counts as the two gates it really costs. The four classic styles give the plain textbook forms.

    Optimize

    • Optimize on the top bar shortens the cable runs on any bench, whether you wired it by hand or the solver built it. It may move modules to other slots (empty ones too) and turn them 180°. It can also move a gate's job to an identical gate on the same or another chip of the same type, swap the inputs of symmetric gates, and re-lay every connection as the shortest chain the header pins allow. The circuit stays electrically the same. Modules that gain nothing from moving stay where they are, and Undo restores the old layout.
    • The design builder runs the same optimizer after a build when Optimize placement and cable runs is ticked. It fills one chip before starting the next, and it uses spare gates on chips it already needs (for example, a spare XOR with H as an inverter) before adding a module. In Settings you can stop it turning modules, or keep every gate where it is so that only modules move and cables are re-laid.

    Printing a lab sheet

    • The little receipt printer on top of the floppy drive makes a one-page landscape sheet of what is on the bench, in two columns, shrunk to fit the page. It shows where each module goes (M1–M12, and whether it's turned), the truth table measured on the bench (or a clock-by-clock sequence for counters), the logic diagram, and a numbered wiring list. The list has one row per cable with a tick box, both ends (module, pin number and pin name) and the cable colour. It's grouped into constants, inputs and chip outputs, and follows each signal along its chain, so you can wire the real case from top to bottom. Choose "Save as PDF" in the print dialog to keep a copy.

    The case

    • Saving and loading happen on the floppy drive on the table behind the module rack. SAVE writes the bench to a file and LOAD reads one back (dropping a file on the page works too). Click the drive itself to copy the bench as text or paste one in. The floppies next to it hold the examples: click one to put it in the drive's slot, then press LOAD to load it (it replaces the bench, and Undo brings yours back). Click the floppy in the slot to take it back out.
    • Press either latch on the front of the case to close the lid, and press it again to open it. While the lid is shut, the bench ignores clicks, but the circuit keeps running if MAINS is on.

    Camera

    • Right-drag to orbit, middle-drag to pan, scroll to zoom. That works over the sheets and this pamphlet too. On touch screens, use two fingers. This pamphlet lies on the table to the right of the case.
